FakeGuard Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 4, 2026

FakeGuard is a browser extension and app that checks pictures and videos to help estimate whether they're AI-generated or otherwise faked. This page explains what FakeGuard does with your data, plainly and completely.

The short version

FakeGuard checks content in several ways. Most checks run entirely on your device. One check — comparing content against fact-checkers and prior appearances online — sends some information to outside services (Google's Fact Check API and a reverse-image search service) and may visit a few web pages where the same content appeared. It's on by default; you can turn it off in FakeGuard's settings and still get results from every other check.

What happens fully on your device (nothing sent anywhere)

Most of FakeGuard's checks run entirely on your device and never send anything anywhere: the frequency-pattern check (DCT), the learned-look classifier (MobileNet), the camera-sensor-fingerprint check (PRNU), all of the biological/physiological checks (heartbeat pattern, eye behavior, lip-sync), the local metadata/C2PA reading, the comparison of a picture against copies of itself, the plausibility check, and the local cache FakeGuard keeps so it doesn't re-check the same content twice.

What does get sent off your device, and why

When this is on (it's on by default, and you can turn it off), FakeGuard may send the web address of the image or video thumbnail you're viewing, along with any nearby caption or claim text, to two outside services:

  • Google's Fact Check API — used to check whether this content has already been fact-checked by an independent source. This receives the caption or claim text, or the image's web address.
  • A reverse-image search provider (Apify) — used to check whether this exact content has appeared online before, possibly in a different context. This receives the image's web address, and the service then fetches the picture from that address; FakeGuard never uploads the picture file itself. If the search suggests the content was used out of context, FakeGuard may send the matching page's address to the same provider for a second check.

FakeGuard may also open up to three of the web pages that search found, and up to six pictures on each, to look for an original copy of the image with its source information intact. These are ordinary web requests, made from the connection FakeGuard is running on (in the browser extension, yours). The websites can see that a request was made, such as your internet address and browser type, as with any web visit; nothing about you or the file you are viewing is sent to them.

This is used only to perform these checks. No other part of FakeGuard sends anything off your device.

Your control over this

This behavior is on by default, but you can turn it off at any time in FakeGuard's settings. If you turn it off, you'll still get a result from every other check FakeGuard runs — you'll just lose the fact-checking and reverse-search parts of the score.

What FakeGuard does not do

FakeGuard does not sell your data, does not use what it sees for advertising, and does not store the actual images or videos you check — only a short fingerprint (a hash) and the resulting score, kept temporarily to avoid re-checking the same content, with older entries automatically deleted once a size limit is reached.
